Understanding Mental Health

A plain-language library of mental health conditions — what they are, signs, causes, treatment, and where to find help. Not a diagnosis.

Mood disorders affect how a person feels emotionally over time — beyond ordinary sadness or ups and downs.

Conditions covered

  • Depression
  • Bipolar disorder
  • Disruptive mood dysregulation disorder (DMDD)

Signs & symptoms

  • Persistent low mood or loss of interest
  • Changes in sleep, appetite, or energy
  • Periods of unusually high or irritable mood (bipolar)
  • Difficulty concentrating or functioning

Causes & risk factors

Genetics, brain chemistry, stressful life events, trauma, chronic illness, and social factors can all play a role.

Treatment & support

Talking therapy, medication (prescribed by a professional), lifestyle changes, and family support.

When to seek help

When symptoms last more than two weeks, affect daily life, or include thoughts of self-harm.

Myth: Depression is just laziness or a lack of faith.
Fact: Depression is a real, treatable health condition — not a character flaw.

Anxiety disorders involve intense, persistent worry or fear that interferes with daily life.

Conditions covered

  • Generalised anxiety disorder
  • Social anxiety
  • Panic disorder
  • Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD)

Signs & symptoms

  • Constant worry or overthinking
  • Racing heart, sweating, trembling
  • Avoiding situations out of fear
  • Repetitive thoughts or behaviours (OCD)

Treatment & support

Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT), relaxation skills, medication where appropriate, and peer support.

Myth: You can just "snap out of it".
Fact: Anxiety disorders are treatable — but they usually need real support, not willpower alone.

Conditions that affect how the brain develops and functions, often noticeable in childhood.

Conditions covered

  • Autism spectrum disorder
  • Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)
  • Intellectual disability

Signs & symptoms

  • Differences in communication and social interaction (autism)
  • Difficulty sustaining attention, impulsivity, restlessness (ADHD)
  • Delayed developmental milestones

Support

Early assessment, speech/occupational therapy, educational support, and family coaching.

Myth: ADHD means a child is just badly behaved.
Fact: ADHD is a recognised neurodevelopmental condition that responds to support and structure.

Conditions that affect a person's sense of reality — including thoughts, perceptions, and behaviour.

Conditions covered

  • Schizophrenia
  • Related psychotic experiences

Signs & symptoms

  • Hallucinations (hearing or seeing things others don't)
  • Delusions (strong beliefs not shared by others)
  • Disorganised thinking or speech
  • Withdrawal and changes in functioning

Treatment

Psychiatric assessment, medication, family support, and psychosocial rehabilitation. Early treatment improves outcomes.

Myth: Psychosis is a spiritual attack that only prayer can fix.
Fact: Spiritual support can help — but psychosis needs medical care. Both can work together.

Challenges with alcohol, drugs, or behaviours that become compulsive and harmful.

Conditions covered

  • Alcohol use disorder
  • Cannabis use disorder
  • Other substance-related challenges

Signs & symptoms

  • Difficulty stopping or cutting down
  • Cravings and withdrawal
  • Neglecting responsibilities
  • Continued use despite harm

Support

Detox where needed, counselling, peer recovery groups, family involvement, and rehabilitation centres.

Myth: Addiction is a moral failure.
Fact: Addiction is a health condition — recovery is possible with the right support.

Conditions that appear in childhood and affect behaviour, emotions, or development.

Conditions covered

  • Encopresis
  • Enuresis
  • Oppositional defiant disorder (ODD)
  • Conduct disorder (CD)
  • Selective mutism

Support

Child assessment, parenting support, school collaboration, and therapy.

Myth: A difficult child just needs stricter discipline.
Fact: Behavioural conditions often need understanding and professional support — not punishment.

Is This What I Am Feeling?

You don't need a diagnosis to deserve support. These everyday experiences are common — and you are not alone.

Why do I feel lonely even when surrounded by people?You can be surrounded and still feel unseen. Belonging is about connection, not company.
Why am I always overthinking?Overthinking is often the mind trying to feel safe. It can be calmed with practice and support.
Why do I struggle to say no?Boundaries are a skill. Struggling with them often comes from fear of disappointing others.
Why do I feel emotionally exhausted?Emotional exhaustion can follow prolonged stress, caregiving, or unresolved pain.
Why am I afraid of being abandoned?Fear of abandonment often traces back to early experiences. It can heal.
Why do I feel guilty when I prioritise myself?Self-care can feel selfish when you've learned to put others first. It isn't.
Why do I feel like I am never good enough?That voice is not the truth about you. It can be challenged with support.
Why do I feel empty even when things seem fine?Emptiness can signal unmet emotional needs or unresolved grief. You deserve care.

The Support Corner

Practical coping strategies, self-care, and pathways to professional help.

🌬️

Grounding & breathing

Simple 4-7-8 breathing and 5-4-3-2-1 grounding exercises to calm the nervous system in moments of distress.

🛏️

Sleep & routine

Consistent sleep and wake times, reduced screens before bed, and gentle daily structure can stabilise mood.

📓

Journaling

Writing down thoughts can reduce their intensity. Try three lines a day: what I felt, what I need, what I can do.

🤝

Asking for help

You don't need to explain everything perfectly. "I'm struggling and I'd like support" is enough to begin.

🚶

Movement

A short walk, stretching, or dancing can shift mood. Movement is medicine for the mind.

📵

Digital boundaries

Unfollow accounts that make you feel small. Curate your feed like you curate your space.
